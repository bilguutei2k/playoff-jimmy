# Is Playoff Jimmy real?

Episode ID: `playoff-jimmy`

## Question and purpose

Does Jimmy Butler's scoring improve in the playoffs, how much of the observed
change is associated with playing time, and how does the pattern compare with
Jamal Murray across seasons?

Make the viewer understand why per-game scoring, scoring rate and efficiency
can tell different stories. Establish whether an exceptional series describes
a whole playoff run or a recurring pattern. Do not prescribe a winner or a
"myth debunked" ending.

## Scope

Decided 2026-09-28: Butler's paired seasons from 2011–12 and Murray's from
2016–17, both through 2025–26, compared with a peer benchmark of high-minute
players in the same seasons. `METHOD.md` holds the pre-registered rules:
season sets, benchmark, wording bands and the conditions for any "real"
verdict. Each player is compared within his own seasons; the shared-season
comparison is shown beside each player's full record, never instead of it.

Use the 2022–23 postseason, which ended with the two players meeting in the
Finals, as the potential opening case study; the earlier research is a lead to
verify, not a finding. Analyze complete coverage before selecting examples for
the film. Include seasons where a player's scoring declines; absence from the
playoffs is missing exposure, not zero performance.

Primary endpoint: change in points per 36 minutes, placed against the peer
benchmark. Also report points per game, minutes per game, shot volume and
approximate true shooting. Series detail is limited to 2022–23. This is an
investigation of scoring, not a complete ranking of basketball ability or
proof of a psychological "clutch" effect.

## Audience and delivery

Audience, language and final voice remain open. Research and provisional scene
descriptions can proceed in English; this does not select a final narration
language. Aim for the shortest film that fairly explains the result. A 7–10
minute range is a planning reference, not a required duration.

Use original data graphics as the initial visual approach. The user's Opus
examples will guide the final design. No paid audio service or footage purchase
is authorized. Silent or captioned drafts must be labeled as such.

## Required deliverables

- First research milestone: frozen accessible inputs, a coverage audit,
  executable paired-season calculations and an evidence-linked result packet.
- First production milestone: an evidence-linked storyboard and one 30–45
  second representative segment, visually inspected at the intended size.
- Subsequent production: a long-form rough cut; two or three independently
  understandable vertical adaptations if the findings support them; a local
  interactive companion comparing seasons and metrics.
- Final video, final narration and public publication are not claimed complete
  by a research report, code compilation or a silent draft render.

## Boundaries and decisions

Local work only. Preserve the current hypothesis even if the attractive
initial narrative fails. Record source coverage failures explicitly. Do not
silently turn a few famous runs into a career-wide conclusion.

Decisions already made: Playoff Jimmy is the first project; Murray is a useful
comparison; prioritize analysis and video before building an elaborate website.
The partner should challenge the method before a complete script is written.

If source access supports only a narrow historical case study, deliver the
verified bounded analysis and coverage gap. Defer the broader conclusion;
continue reusable production work only with clear provisional labeling.
